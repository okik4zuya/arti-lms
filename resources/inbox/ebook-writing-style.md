Extracted from `inovelty/FINAL EBOOK - SCOPUS BLUEPRINT MASTERY.md` (see [[inovelty-reference]] for
where the source file lives and which *content* ideas were adapted). This file is about *how it's
written*, not what it says — intended as a prose/formatting template for the ARTi Workflow ebook.
No text was copied verbatim; this is a pattern analysis.

## Overall voice

- Bahasa Indonesia, formal-akademik but warm — not stiff textbook Indonesian. Sits between a
  university module and a coaching book.
- Second person throughout: "Anda" (formal you), never "kamu" in the main body — except inside
  short informal asides/rhetorical stretches (see "Register shifts" below), where "kamu" leaks in
  for a few paragraphs to sound more like a mentor talking casually. This is an inconsistency in
  the source, not a deliberate device — pick one register and hold it for ARTi's ebook rather than
  copying the drift.
- First-person plural used sparingly for shared framing: "Mari kita mulai", "kita akan bedah
  langkah demi langkah".
- Confident, declarative sentence openers stating a claim, then unpacking it: "X bukan sekadar Y.
  X adalah Z." This bukan-sekadar / adalah contrast pair recurs constantly as the core rhetorical
  move (see "Signature sentence patterns").

## Structural skeleton (per chapter/BAGIAN)

1. **Cold open reframe** (1-3 short paragraphs): states the common misconception or naive approach
   before introducing the correct concept. Almost every section opens by describing what people
   *usually* do wrong before presenting the fix.
2. **Why-it-matters paragraph**: explains the stakes (desk rejection, weak novelty, reviewer
   comments) in concrete terms.
3. **Core concept explained**, usually via:
   - a numbered list of components/steps, or
   - a small comparison table (2-4 columns), or
   - a named framework/diagram introduced with "Gambar N. [Name]" followed by a caption paragraph
     explaining how to read it (axes, quadrants, cycle stages).
4. **Sub-typology breakdown**: when a concept has variants (5 types of novelty, 4 types of gap),
   each gets its own numbered mini-section: bold/numbered heading, 1-3 short paragraphs, often a
   bullet list of "muncul ketika..." (arises when...) trigger conditions, then a closing sentence
   stating the payoff or risk.
5. **Common-mistakes callout**: a short subsection or table titled "Kesalahan Umum" listing
   mistake → dampak (impact) pairs.
6. **Practical instrument**: a checklist (☐ bullet items), worksheet (fill-in-the-blank table), or
   fill-in template (bracketed placeholders like `[topik/teori utama]`) that operationalizes the
   chapter's concept.
7. **Interpretation/scoring rule** immediately after a checklist: a one-line threshold rule such as
   "Jika lebih dari 3 poin belum tercentang, maka..." — always phrased as a conditional consequence,
   never a bare score.
8. **Closing bridge paragraph**: 1-2 sentences that summarize the point just made and gesture to
   the next section, e.g. "Namun pemahaman konseptual saja tidak cukup... Maka dari itu, bagian
   berikutnya hadir sebagai alat bantu praktis."

## Signature sentence patterns

- **Contrast redefinition**: "[Common term] bukan sekadar [naive definition]. [Term] adalah
  [correct definition]." e.g. "Gap adalah kekosongan dalam ruang literatur... Novelty adalah
  kontribusi baru yang Anda hadirkan."
- **Rhetorical question openers** for a section or sub-idea: "Mengapa X sangat krusial?", "Apa
  yang Dimaksud Arena Kompetisi?" — posed then answered in the following paragraph(s).
- **Direct-address hypotheticals in the prelude/intro only**: "Bagaimana jika saya mengatakan
  kepada Anda, bahwa..." — repeated 2-3 times as an opening hook device, not used elsewhere in the
  book. Reserve this move for the ebook's own prelude/foreword, not body chapters.
- **Short punchy one-line paragraphs for emphasis**, especially after a build-up: "Di sinilah
  banyak paper ditolak." / "Itu bukan novelty. Itu hanya celah konteks." These stand alone,
  surrounded by blank lines, never buried mid-paragraph.
- **Simple ≠ list restated as equation**: "Sederhananya: Gap = masalah yang ditemukan. Novelty =
  solusi atau pembaruan yang Anda tawarkan." Reduces an abstract distinction to a compact formula
  after explaining it in prose.
- **Concrete example immediately after abstract claim**, always labeled explicitly: "Contoh
  sederhana:" then two contrasting quoted mini-examples (a weak version and a strong version)
  presented back-to-back for direct comparison.
- **Enumerated diagnostic questions** ("Tanyakan pada diri Anda:") used to operationalize an
  abstract judgment call (e.g. determining if research is "incremental atau breakthrough").

## Register shifts (found in Part 4 on novelty types)

A few sub-sections shift to a more casual, spoken-language register: "kamu" instead of "Anda",
contractions ("nggak"), filler transitions ("Tapi ya,", "Jadi bukan sekadar..."), and second-person
imperative asides. This reads like a different pass/author voice bleeding through — inconsistent
with the rest of the book. **For ARTi's ebook, do not replicate this inconsistency** — commit to
one register (formal "Anda") throughout, or make a deliberate, signaled register shift (e.g. a
clearly boxed "quick take" aside) rather than letting it drift paragraph to paragraph.

## Lists, tables, and checklists — formatting conventions

- Checklists use ☐ (empty checkbox glyph) as bullet, one short first-person self-assessment
  statement per line, grouped under a bold sub-label (e.g. "Fase Strategic (Arena & Positioning)").
- Every checklist is followed by an **interpretation rule**, never left to speak for itself:
  "Jika lebih dari 3 poin belum tercentang, maka penelitian Anda berisiko mengalami mismatch..."
- Comparison tables are small (2-6 rows, 2-3 columns) and always given a numbered caption above
  them: "Tabel N. [Descriptive name]" — followed immediately by a prose paragraph interpreting the
  table, not just presenting it raw.
- Diagrams are captioned "Gambar N. [Name]" and always followed by a structured explanation of how
  to read them — axes named and defined, quadrants/stages enumerated with a short definition each,
  then a closing "how to use this" paragraph tying it back to the reader's own decision-making.
- Fill-in templates use bracketed placeholders in flowing prose (not just blank tables), e.g. a
  full contribution-statement paragraph template: "Penelitian ini berkontribusi pada literatur
  mengenai [topik/teori utama] dengan cara memperluas pemahaman tentang [konsep utama...]." —
  the placeholder sits inside a complete, grammatically correct sentence the reader edits in place.
- Numbered lists inside a sub-typology explanation typically end with a short reflective/critical
  qualifier sentence: "Namun penting untuk dipahami: tidak semua perbedaan konteks otomatis menjadi
  kontribusi ilmiah." Never presents a taxonomy as pure positive without a caveat on misuse.

## Recurring lexicon / terms of art

Domain vocabulary that carries the book's argument (useful as a checklist of "does ARTi's ebook
also define and use these consistently" terms): positioning, arena kompetisi, diskursus (ilmiah),
kontribusi (teoretis/metodologis/kontekstual/empiris/integratif), pseudo-gap / gap semu, framing,
blueprint, breakthrough, reverse engineering, argument flow, alur konvensional vs proaktif/reaktif.
Note the pattern: every abstract noun gets an adjectival qualifier pinning it to a category
(teoretis, metodologis, empiris, konseptual) — abstractions are rarely left unqualified.

## Chapter/part naming convention

- Top level: "FASE N [NAME]" (e.g. "FASE 1 STRATEGIC") groups 2-3 "BAGIAN" (chapters).
- Chapter level: "BAGIAN N" followed on its own line by a short descriptive title in title case
  ("Structured Research Gap Mapping").
- Each Fase opens with a one-page mini-TOC listing just its own Bagian titles before the content
  starts.
- Section headers within a chapter are ALL CAPS, short, imperative/topical phrases (e.g. "GAP VS
  NOVELTY (JANGAN TERTUKAR)", "MENYUSUN ARGUMENT FLOW", "5 BENTUK NOVELTY DALAM JURNAL
  INTERNASIONAL") — often phrased as a task the reader is about to do, not a neutral topic label.

## What to deliberately NOT copy

- The register drift between formal "Anda" and casual "kamu" (pick one and hold it).
- Front-matter overhead (copyright/disclaimer boilerplate, "Penerbit/Brand" pages) — irrelevant to
  ARTi's own front matter design.
- Any of Inovelty's own branded terms (SBP Framework, Strategic–Breakthrough–Publication, SBP
  Advanced Mentoring) — these are their IP; ARTi has its own Amati-Replikasi-Tambah inovasi
  framing (see [[arti-philosophy]]) to carry the same structural role.
